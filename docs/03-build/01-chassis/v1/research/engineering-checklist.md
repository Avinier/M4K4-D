## 1. Wheel-to-chassis mechanism — first work package

The intended paths are **floor → tyre → wheel rim/web → stub shaft → bearing pair → bearing housing and motor carrier → cheeks/rails/deck → body**, and **motor output shaft → stub shaft → wheel** for torque. Both paths must be explicit; the motor's output bearing must not silently become the main wheel support.

Working file: [axle stack](axle-stack.md) (coupling, retention, bearing stack), accepted as [D-007](../../../decisions.md#d-007) and modelled; not quoted, printed or tested. The [motor interface sheet](motor-interface-sheet.md) is parked for future hardware receipt, not an active design task.

The [review holds](axle-stack.md#6-proposal-review-holds-and-their-status) are fixed in CAD except outward inner-ring retention, fits, the stub quote and the alignment proof, which stay open until coupons and the axle rig. No motor is available now; use documented supplier/CAD assumptions and keep motor-contacting parts adjustable or replaceable. Do not mark a physical fit as verified.

- [x] **Define the replaceable motor interface.** *Closed in CAD by D-007: the replaceable 5 mm plate-and-cheek carrier carries the assumed motor face pattern; 0.3 mm nominal float at the pilot/face screws lets the motor align to the independently supported stub, and screws are tightened last through housing key slots. Fit against the actual motor remains open.* Keep the motor-specific geometry in this removable carrier; verify assumed hole pattern, adjustment allowance, tool access and correction method when hardware arrives.
- [x] **Select a manufacturable stub shaft and torque coupling.** *Defined in CAD by D-007: turned EN8/C45 stub, Ø4.05 H8 round bore with 8 mm shaft engagement, and DIN 916 M3 × 2.5 cup-point set screw bearing on the motor D-flat. The set screw is the selected torque coupling; its slip/loosening resistance is unproven. Open: dimensioned stub drawing and shop quote.* Verify the actual shaft/flat, insertion depth, concentricity and release access; run the reversal test before closing the physical gate.
- [x] **Close wheel-to-stub attachment.** *Closed in CAD by D-007/D-008: Ø10 spigot centres the wheel, three M3 × 6 (CH-056) into the stub flange, hub cap covers them; hardware is in the BOM. Web creep and the cap fit are for the coupon.* Specify how the printed rim/web carries torque without slipping or cracking, how the wheel is axially retained, and whether the same fastener also retains the motor coupling. Add every washer, clamp, screw, nut, or insert to the BOM.

Retain the rings between housing shoulder and cap without shield contact. Run the 100 N outward-hold test; this standalone bearing/stub fixture does not need a motor. Do not infer load capacity from generic listing copy.*
- [ ] **Engineer the motor carrier and removable plate.** *CAD architecture is now modeled: one plate-and-cheek carrier per side with a bolt-on bearing housing. - [x] **Define the carrier-to-frame joints (J04).** *J04 is defined in CAD (2026-09-29): one printed plate-and-cheek carrier per side keys into the front and rear rail ends; four M3 × 8 screws and four 6 mm rail inserts clamp each carrier. ## 2. Print split and whole-chassis joining plan

- [ ] *(Started: frame-split study, nine provisional frame units before the two axle carriers.)* Reconcile every `CH-001` solid into print units: rear and front deck prints (tub walls integral with front deck), four rail segments, front/rear crossmembers, two integrated plate-and-cheek carriers, and separate bottom hatch; add any remaining brackets or service parts. Mark which CAD solids print together and which are assembled after printing.
- [ ] Choose the structural material and printer/process from the 3D lab's actual capability. Record usable build volume, nozzle, enclosure, filament, layer height and orientation limits. PETG is a trial candidate, not a released choice.
- [ ] Lay out the split around the motor relief, battery opening, body mounting points, and crossmembers so the chassis can be assembled without trapping the motors, battery, bearings, or wiring.
- [ ] For every printed part, define print orientation against the main force direction, supports and removal access, minimum walls/ribs, overhangs, inserts, post-processing, and the datum faces used to inspect it.
- [ ] Define alignment between split frame parts: pilot faces, tabs, dowels, or controlled mating surfaces. Screws clamp; they must not be the sole source of location where wheel alignment depends on it.
- [ ] *(Started: J01–J16 are recorded in the [joint register](../joint-register.md).)* Close every open receiver, BOM, fastener, engagement, tolerance, tool-access and proof field; add omitted frame-joint hardware to the project BOM.
- [ ] Reconcile the printed-part mass with the whole-robot mass/CoM register and test ballast plan after the split and material are chosen.
- [ ] Export one printable mesh per custom part with revision/handedness and a matching assembly STEP; verify mesh watertightness, orientation, scale, build-volume fit, and correspondence to source CAD.

## 3. Complete chassis joint register

For **every row**, record: connected parts, load path, locating feature, material/process, fastener standard/length/count, receiver, clamp stack, engagement and edge distance, tolerances, tool clearance, assembly order, removal path, and proof method. A modeled screw position alone is open.

The [chassis v1 joint register](../joint-register.md) is the working interface-by-interface record for J01–J16, including connected parts, load paths, locators, modeled fasteners/receivers, clamp stacks, tolerances, access, service paths, proof methods and unresolved items. It separates “defined in CAD” from physical verification and records CAD/BOM receiver gaps that must be closed before procurement. Add a row whenever the print split creates another interface, and add resulting hardware to the project BOM before release.

Current design status: J01–J03 and J14 are designed or provisionally dimensioned, with received-part fit and physical proof open. J04 is defined in CAD as a keyed carrier-to-rail joint with screws, rail inserts and a documented load path; its fit, insert pull-out and stiffness proof remain open. J05/J06 and J16 are provisional. J07/J08 are defined in CAD, with fit, access and physical tests open. J09A lacks matching receivers; J09B and J10A/B lack retention details. J11A is provisional and missing BOM hardware; J11B lacks a pack restraint. J12 has modeled geometry but incomplete screw specification; J13 has modeled positions but an incomplete clamp stack and access check. J15A is modeled; J15B mounting and cable restraint are incomplete. Use the [joint register](../joint-register.md) for row-level closure gaps; do not treat “designed in CAD” as physically verified.

## 4. Assembly, disassembly, and access check

- [ ] Write a numbered assembly sequence from bare printed parts through rails/crossmembers, J04 carriers, motors, bearing housings/caps, stubs/wheels, caster pod, skid, battery tub, sensors, cables, ballast and body interface. For each step, show the required tool, fastener installation direction and reachable side.
- [ ] Complete reverse service procedures for **one motor**, **one wheel/tyre**, **one bearing pair**, **the caster**, **the rear sensor/shoe**, and **the battery**. Motor, wheel and bearing removal paths are documented in [axle-stack.md](axle-stack.md#5-assembly-and-service-one-side); the caster has a basic removal sequence in the [joint register](../joint-register.md#j07a-pololu-2691-caster-to-printed-pod). Document rear sensor/shoe and battery service, and consolidate all procedures with every prerequisite removal and tool. Revise geometry if a routine replacement requires dismantling the whole chassis.
- [ ] Check hand/tool access with the body installed and lifted off, including low-head motor-face screws, J04 carrier screws, crossmember screws, pod screws, rear-keel screws, battery connector, hatch fasteners and body M4 nuts.
- [ ] Confirm no fastener becomes captive behind a wheel, tub, panel, or bearing after the next assembly step. Provide nut traps or access windows where needed.
- [ ] Check cable paths and strain relief during removal: motor/encoder leads, front sensor and switch, rear TCRT, IMU, and battery. Set a connector disconnect point before any part is pulled far enough to load its wires.
- [ ] Check that the battery can be isolated and removed without exposing an unprotected conductor or requiring destructive disassembly; coordinate the electrical interface with its separate release.
- [ ] Perform a dry assembly with printed fit parts and the intended tools. Record assembly time, tool collisions, damaged inserts, forced fits, and ambiguous handed parts; revise CAD and instructions.

## 5. Release package check

- [ ] Purchased-interface assumptions, sources, uncertainty allowances and replaceable/adjustable features are reflected in CAD and the BOM; actual motor fit remains an open physical gate until hardware is available.
- [ ] All J01–J16 rows have a selected joint, part number/hardware, CAD representation, and verification method; no load-bearing interface remains an implied fusion of separate CAD solids.
- [ ] The print split, orientation, material, calibrated fits, supports, slicer settings, and per-part exports are recorded.
- [ ] Assembly and service procedures have been dry-run on actual or representative parts.
- [ ] Fit, loaded stiffness, retention, runout, traction/turning, braking/tip, skid/caster, motor temperature, and service-access tests have defined limits and recorded results at representative mass/CoM.
- [ ] Chassis-specific CAD checks and mesh validation are rerun on the release revision; open physical tests remain visibly OPEN.
