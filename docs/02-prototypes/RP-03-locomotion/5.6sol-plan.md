# RP-03: what we are designing

RP-01 asks whether the **head mechanism** can produce its storyboarded poses and movements. RP-02 asks whether the **electrical and control backbone** survives the operating states and faults. RP-03 asks whether the **mobile base** can move with the intended character while remaining stable, stoppable, and locally safe.

Its central question is:

> Can a two-wheel differential-drive base, carrying Makad’s representative mass and centre of mass, approach, retreat, turn, spin, creep, and stop on approved indoor floors—while its own controller enforces limits, obstacle protection, tabletop inhibition, and fault responses?

The output is more than a chassis drawing or a motor choice. It is a **validated motion envelope**: which movements are allowed, on which surfaces, at which speeds and loads, with what stopping distance and stability margin.

## What is already fixed, and what RP-03 must determine

The [dimensional baseline](/Users/avinier/robotics/makad/docs/01-system/dimensional-baseline.md) v1.12 has already chosen the broad architecture: **two independently powered encoder wheels, a front ball transfer, and a mandatory rear anti-tip skid**. A swivel caster remains the RP-03 comparison swap. Its planning geometry is:

| Parameter | Current baseline |
|---|---:|
| Overall size target | 300 H × 205 W × 180 D mm |
| Drive wheels | Ø84 mm nominal; Ø80–85 mm acceptable |
| Wheel track | ~170 mm centre to centre |
| Drive axle to front-support contact | ~110 mm |
| Rear-skid reach / height | ~70 mm behind axle / ≤14 mm above floor |
| Normal travel | ~0.15–0.40 m/s |
| Fast expressive travel | ~0.40–0.60 m/s |
| Drivetrain capability target | ~0.65–0.70 m/s |
| Following trial limit | **≤0.5 m/s** |

The **exact motors, gearboxes, encoders, wheels, tire material, ball-transfer SKU, skid, motor driver, base controller, obstacle sensors, edge sensors, and control limits** remain to be chosen and validated. The front-support *type* is the ball transfer (BD-08).

There is a particularly important open assumption: the whole-robot centre-of-mass target is `x=+25 mm` forward of the axle and `h=124 mm` above the floor. The current head is substantially heavier than the early concept, so those coordinates need to be recalculated from the actual component layout. They cannot simply be carried into a motor or acceleration decision.

# The seven RP-03 workstreams

These are the *kinds* of work, analogous to the seven RP-02 bifurcations.

## 1. Motion storyboard and operating cases

This is the direct analogue of RP-01’s head storyboard. We should define the base movements from **what the audience should see** and **what the hardware must survive**, then quantify each.

The vocabulary needs at least:

| Motion | Character intent | Engineering case |
|---|---|---|
| Stationary attentive | Makad feels anchored while its head and face act | Wheel creep, vibration, hold current, head-induced rocking |
| Come/approach | Interested approach and respectful stop | Acceleration, straight tracking, braking into the 0.6–0.9 m person band |
| Follow segment | Moves with a person without crowding | ≤0.5 m/s, gentle arc, interruption and target loss |
| Creep/reposition | Small deliberate adjustment | Minimum controllable speed, stiction, encoder resolution |
| Retreat/recoil | Brief backward response | Reverse sensing, rear clearance, caster reversal, skid behavior |
| Orienting turn | Turns its body toward something | Differential wheel speeds, turn accuracy, caster behavior |
| Excited spin | Energetic flourish with a readable ending | Turn scrub, peak current, slip, body rocking and settling |
| Obstacle encounter | Stops before harmful contact | Detection coverage, latency, braking distance, blind regions |
| Physical stop | Hazardous motion loses permission | Actual coast/brake distance after motor cutoff; no restart on release |
| Tabletop demonstration | Head/face remain active; base stays still | Default motion inhibition |
| Tabletop calibration | Tiny explicitly enabled motion | Edge detection, complete footprint, catch platform, validated radius |
| Fault settle | Predictable response to lost sensing/control | Timeout, sensor loss, reset, brownout, fresh-intent recovery |

Each storyboard panel needs more than `move 300 mm`. It should name:

- floor or tabletop mode and the surface;
- initial position, heading, head pose, mass and CoM case;
- path or angle, speed and shaped acceleration/deceleration;
- obstacles, edges and required valid sensors;
- what interrupts the phrase;
- the final footprint and settling behavior;
- the minimum viable and expressive versions, where they impose different loads.

For example, **“come here”** becomes a sequence: accept a fresh target while stationary → align or begin a smooth arc → travel only while stopping clearance exists → start braking early enough to enter the 0.6–0.9 m band → settle without rollback or an unrequested second creep. RP-03 can test that against a marked target. RP-07 later supplies real person tracking and reacquisition.

That sequencing matters. A maximum speed figure alone cannot tell us whether Makad can start gently, stop respectfully, or abandon the approach when an obstacle appears.

## 2. Chassis mechanics and motion physics

This workstream converts the storyboard into motor, tire, support, and structural requirements.

At Ø84 mm, **0.70 m/s requires about 159 wheel RPM**. At a 170 mm track, a **220°/s in-place turn requires about 74 RPM per wheel** in opposite directions. Those are wheel speeds; a candidate gearbox must deliver them *under load and at the low usable supply voltage*, with margin.

Then calculate each maneuver’s demand:

- acceleration force from whole-robot mass;
- rolling resistance, caster resistance, tire scrub and any allowed slope;
- wheel torque and gearbox/motor torque at the corresponding speed;
- continuous, repeated and short peak current;
- braking torque and regenerative behavior;
- heat over repeated motion phrases.

The stability calculation is equally central. With the provisional `+25 mm / 124 mm` CoM, the simple forward-acceleration caster-lift estimate is approximately **1.98 m/s²**. That is a *theoretical onset estimate*, not a permitted acceleration. The rear skid’s 70 mm reach and ≤14 mm height were chosen around that model. RP-03 must measure lift and skid contact with representative ballast and head poses, then set a command limit below measured onset with a registered margin.

We also need separate checks for forward braking, reverse acceleration, lateral loading during turns, ball-cup jam and denting, tire slip, and the support triangle formed by the two wheels and front ball. A stable static CAD pose does not prove a stable excited spin.

## 3. Local control and motion safety

RP-02 defines the larger power, link, watchdog and restart architecture. RP-03 must implement the **base-specific local controller** within that architecture.

The base MCU should own encoder capture, wheel-speed control, acceleration and speed clamps, command expiry, obstacle and edge inhibition, motor permission, fault reporting, and safe boot behavior. The Raspberry Pi may decide *where* to go; it must not be able to bypass local motion limits.

The important contracts are concrete:

- What command does the SBC send: bounded `v` and yaw rate, a short trajectory, or a goal?
- How long is that command valid?
- What happens if one encoder stops reporting?
- What happens if an obstacle sensor is unavailable?
- What happens if the SBC freezes while the wheels are moving?
- What happens when the motor rail drops but logic remains alive?
- What state follows E-stop release, app reconnect, or controller reboot?

The safe recovery rule from RP-02 carries through: **restoring power or a link does not resume the old motion**. The base needs a new arm and fresh intent.

## 4. Candidate sourcing

Sourcing should compare **complete drive sets**, not isolated catalog motors. A useful candidate is a motor, gearbox, encoder, wheel, driver, supply interface and mount that work together inside this chassis.

The screen should include:

- torque–speed curve at the relevant voltage, continuous and peak current, thermal behavior;
- gearbox ratio, efficiency, backlash and output-shaft load capacity;
- encoder counts per wheel revolution and low-speed measurement quality;
- wheel loaded radius, tread grip, turn scrub, runout and shaft retention;
- caster trail, swivel envelope, starting force and flutter;
- driver current limit, fault output, braking/coasting and regeneration behavior;
- sensor coverage, latency and blind regions on *this* chassis;
- dimensions, installed mass, mounting, service access, supplier, substitute, landed cost and lead time.

There should be at least two credible sets where practical. A motor with an impressive stall-torque number can still fail on loaded speed, heat, gearbox life, wheel fit, noise or stopping behavior. The detailed comparison belongs in RP-03; dated price and availability belong in the existing [candidate-sourcing matrix](/Users/avinier/robotics/makad/docs/01-system/candidate-sourcing-matrix.md).

## 5. Parametric chassis CAD

The CAD should develop in three passes.

**First: envelope and datum model.** Establish the axle-centred coordinate system, wheel contacts, front ball transfer, rear skid, ground plane and overall envelope. Add blockouts for motors, battery low and forward, Pi/power hardware, speaker, microphones, neck/head, wiring and service access. Sweep the front-support pocket through its service removal, rather than checking only the installed pose. The caster comparison article must still fit the same pocket.

**Second: adjustable prototype chassis.** Build an open rig that can move ballast to realistic mass and CoM positions, swap caster/skid candidates, expose test points and keep the physical stop accessible. This is the chassis used to learn the dynamics; it does not need a polished shell.

**Third: candidate-specific package.** Replace generic blockouts with sourced part geometry, fasteners, wire routes and actual masses. Recalculate whole-robot CoM, support geometry, clearances and service removal. That package becomes RP-06’s integrated-layout input.

The CAD freeze condition comes from the approved constraints: mass, volume, heat, power, wiring, stability and moving clearances must all have estimates. An attractive shell cannot substitute for these checks.

## 6. Instrumented rig and qualification

The prototype needs an **open, ballasted, observable chassis**, not just a driving demo. Record command and measured wheel speeds, position/heading, motor voltage/current, sensor raw data and validity, controller mode, fault state, body pitch/roll, lift/skid contact, stopping trigger, and final chassis footprint. External video should be time-aligned with the controller logs.

Tests need named floor surfaces and mass/CoM extremes. Tabletop edge tests require a physical catch so a failed test cannot drop the robot. Controlled braking and physical motor cutoff need separate measurements because cutoff may leave a longer coast.

The governing [RP-03 plan](/Users/avinier/robotics/makad/docs/01-system/risk-prototype-plan.md) already defines six gates:

| Gate | What it establishes |
|---|---|
| **G01 Floor control** | Forward, reverse, turn, stop and settle across registered floor/load cases without instability |
| **G02 Speed boundary** | Slow regulation and enforced limits, including ≤0.5 m/s following trials |
| **G03 Obstacle safety** | Detection and stopping/contact policy for registered obstacles and approach speeds |
| **G04 Tabletop safety** | Default inhibition and any explicitly permitted edge-tested motion within a registered footprint |
| **G05 Fault containment** | Bounded response to stale commands, sensor/link loss, restart and physical stop |
| **G06 Expressive feasibility** | At least one turn/spin/reversal profile with smooth onset and stable settling |

The exact pass numbers for stopping, drift, lift margin, obstacle clearance and tabletop radius are still open. Pilot runs can inform them; the scored thresholds must be registered **before** scored runs.

## 7. Decisions and handoffs

Passing RP-03 provisionally selects the drive/support geometry and local sensing arrangement for **ADR-04 and ADR-07**. It also produces measured values needed elsewhere:

- RP-02 receives real drive current, transients, braking/regeneration, thermal load and energy profiles. New drive loads trigger its coexistence check.
- RP-04 receives safe base-motion primitives and measured onset/settle behavior to coordinate with head, face and sound.
- RP-06 receives the candidate chassis CAD, installed masses, clearances and service envelope.
- RP-07 receives the speed, stopping, sensing and inhibition envelope for actual person following.
- The shared mass and dimensional baselines get explicit updates if measurements require them.

RP-03 can demonstrate a marked-target approach; **RP-07** proves following a person. RP-03 can demonstrate a physically smooth spin; **RP-04** proves it reads as one coordinated character performance. RP-03 measures drive energy; the integrated robot still has to meet the **20-minute** battery criterion.

# The execution order

The seven workstreams overlap, but the practical order is:

1. Freeze the motion storyboard and forbidden situations.
2. Build representative whole-robot mass, CoM, surface and head-pose cases.
3. Calculate wheel speed, torque, current, stability and stopping envelopes.
4. Define the local controller, mode and fault contracts.
5. Screen sourced drive, support and sensor sets against those requirements.
6. Build the parametric envelope and adjustable open chassis.
7. Run pilots, then register measurable G01–G06 thresholds.
8. Run scored maneuver, obstacle, tabletop and fault tests.
9. Iterate the candidate or design where evidence fails.
10. Record the provisional selection and propagate measured budgets.

The relationship to RP-01 is close: **storyboard → physical demand → candidate mechanism → CAD/rig → measured gates → decision**. RP-03 adds the environment and footprint to every motion. The same wheel movement can be acceptable on a clear floor, prohibited near an unseen rear obstacle, and prohibited by default on a tabletop. That is why the storyboard, mode rules and stopping model have to be designed together.
