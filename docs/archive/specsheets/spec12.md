# SCOPE BOUNDARY — append to §1

The availability of the mobile base does **not** transfer expressive locomotion into SPEC-12.

The following are **authored transients** and belong to the performance layer, SPEC-13:

- spins,
- dramatic turns,
- turn-toward gestures,
- startle retreats,
- curious approaches,
- intentional approach toward a person/object,
- intentional retreat from a person/object,
- choreographed repositioning,
- and other semantically meaningful locomotion gestures.

SPEC-12 does not define their:

- trajectory,
- duration,
- amplitude,
- timing,
- choreography,
- semantic trigger,
- or performance asset representation.

SPEC-12 owns only a possible:

> **continuous-in-availability but event-based, stochastic, low-amplitude procedural contribution to the mobile base.**

Because the physical drivetrain has a non-zero minimum controllable speed, the actual base output shall take the form of occasional discrete micro-moves separated by stochastic dwell rather than continuously moving wheels.

C12-R18's prohibition on scripted/repeating procedural animation continues to apply **only to the SPEC-12 contributor**. Deterministic authored base performances remain permitted under SPEC-13.

**SPEC-13 stub pointer:** authored expressive base transients are specified by SPEC-13; this amendment does not define or supersede them.

# §3.8.1 — append after existing neck micro-motion section

## 3.8.1 Procedural mobile-base micro-moves

The procedural base capability is **STRETCH / DEFERRED**.

It is not part of the V1 SPEC-12 floor.

Although the logical channel is continuously available to the procedural architecture, physical drivetrain limitations mean it shall **not** be implemented as continuously drifting \(v\) or \(\omega\).

The base shall reuse the structural timing model already established for gaze:

\[
\boxed{
\text{micro-move}
\rightarrow
\text{dwell}
\rightarrow
\text{micro-move}
\rightarrow
\text{dwell}
}
\]

The locomotion implementation therefore reuses the **saccade-and-dwell concept** rather than introducing a separate continuous-noise model.

The gaze and base channels may have different tuned distributions, but both use the same event/dwell procedural abstraction.

---

## 3.8.1.1 Per-event amplitude ceilings

A procedural translation event shall have integrated signed displacement:

\[
|\Delta s_\text{idle}|
\le
20\text{ mm}
\]

**PROVISIONAL — resolution method: drivetrain bench testing plus observer testing on the assembled Makad.**

A procedural yaw event shall have integrated angular displacement:

\[
|\Delta\psi_\text{idle}|
\le
5^\circ
\]

**PROVISIONAL — resolution method: drivetrain bench testing plus observer testing on the assembled Makad.**

These are **per-event spatial ceilings**, not commanded velocity values.

They exist now so that drivetrain/control architecture has a bounded expressive requirement even though implementation is deferred.

They shall not be interpreted as permission for the procedural layer to accumulate unrestricted travel over multiple events.

---

## 3.8.1.2 Minimum controllable speed

SPEC-16 C16-R24 targets a minimum stable translational command near:

\[
0.05\text{ m/s}
\]

because the drivetrain is expected to encounter:

- stiction,
- gearbox backlash,
- motor deadband,
- and encoder quantisation.

A continuous procedural drift substantially below this velocity is therefore unlikely to produce smooth physical motion.

It is more likely to produce:

- no movement,
- sudden breakaway,
- wheel twitch,
- stick-slip,
- or visibly quantised displacement.

Therefore a non-zero procedural translational micro-move shall use a velocity profile that reaches at least the minimum stable controllable velocity established by SPEC-16.

Similarly, procedural yaw shall not be enabled until the drivetrain has an empirically measured minimum stable in-place/low-radius turning rate.

If no stable controllable yaw rate exists at the required small displacement, procedural yaw micro-moves remain disabled.

The **smallness of idle motion is therefore represented primarily by short displacement and low event frequency, not by commanding arbitrarily tiny wheel velocity.**

---

## 3.8.1.3 Cumulative displacement / mean reversion

Unlike:

- eye offsets,
- expression parameters,
- or neck pose offsets,

base translation integrates into physical position.

An unbiased stochastic base process would therefore become a random walk.

Even individually small events can move Makad a substantial distance over tens of minutes.

When the procedural base layer becomes eligible, it shall latch an odometry-referenced idle origin:

\[
P_0=(x_0,y_0,\psi_0)
\]

and the stochastic micro-move process shall be **mean-reverting toward that origin**.

Outside the neutral region defined by one maximum procedural event, the expected radial component of the next accepted translational micro-move shall point toward \(P_0\), rather than away from it.

The same principle applies to accumulated procedural yaw.

The hard per-event ceiling prevents individual large movements.

The origin bias prevents those small movements from forming an unbounded random walk.

SPEC-16 C16-R54's odometry drift means \(P_0\) is not a perfect world-fixed reference.

SPEC-12 therefore does **not** freeze a tight absolute centimetre-level return-to-origin requirement before C16-R54 is measured.

The permitted long-duration origin error must later be selected using the measured odometry drift envelope.

The return bias is required now; the final absolute displacement tolerance is not.

---

## 3.8.1.4 Audio locomotion inhibit

The procedural base layer shall consume the locomotion-inhibit interface provided through SPEC-16 for audio/listening coordination.

While the inhibit is asserted:

\[
g_\text{base}=0
\]

and no procedural wheel micro-move shall begin or continue.

Suppressed events are discarded.

They are not deferred for later playback.

This creates a genuine limitation:

> If Makad's body microphone array remains in active capture for most of the robot's waking operation, procedural base idle may be eligible only rarely.

SPEC-12 does not assume otherwise.

This limitation reduces the expected expressive value of procedural base idle and contributes directly to its **STRETCH** classification.

---

## 3.8.1.5 Proximity inhibit

Procedural movement has no task-level reason to approach a hazard.

Allowing the safety controller to repeatedly stop tiny idle movements against:

- a wall,
- an object,
- or a table edge

would be collision-safe but visually poor.

Makad would appear to be twitching into a constraint.

Therefore procedural base motion shall be disabled **before** the SPEC-16 safety clamp becomes the normal limiting mechanism.

Let:

\[
d_\text{safety}
\]

be the applicable SPEC-16 obstacle/edge safety-inhibit boundary.

The initial procedural idle inhibit boundary shall be:

\[
d_\text{idle-inhibit}
=
d_\text{safety}
+
50\text{ mm}
\]

**PROVISIONAL — resolution method: completed-base stopping/sensor bench testing plus observer testing near representative obstacles and table edges.**

When detected hazard clearance is less than:

\[
d_\text{idle-inhibit}
\]

then:

\[
g_\text{base}=0
\]

for procedural locomotion.

This is **not** a safety clamp.

SPEC-16 remains the authoritative safety layer.

This earlier inhibit exists to prevent visibly pointless motion into a condition that the safety clamp would predictably reject.

If the required pre-clamp proximity signal is not available, the procedural base layer shall remain disabled.

# §8 — Design-review additions

## 8.10 Procedural base idle is correctly classified STRETCH

The mobile base is mechanically available, but availability alone does not make it a high-value procedural channel.

Four independent constraints reduce its usable operating envelope.

### Cumulative displacement

Unlike eyes and neck offsets, wheel motion integrates into world position.

The generator therefore needs odometry-referenced mean reversion, and the reference itself degrades with odometry drift.

### Minimum controllable speed

A geared differential drive cannot necessarily execute arbitrarily slow expressive drift.

SPEC-16's approximately 0.05 m/s stable-speed floor means subtle motion must instead be represented through short discrete events.

### Audio suppression

Drive noise is structure-borne into Makad's body microphone array.

If active microphone capture dominates waking operation, procedural wheel movement may spend much of its time inhibited.

### Proximity suppression

Near walls or table edges, procedural wheel motion should disappear before the safety clamp has to repeatedly reject it.

These constraints do not make the feature architecturally invalid.

They do make its expected expressive return lower than that of:

- display blink/gaze,
- and probably future neck micro-motion.

Therefore the physical procedural base layer remains **STRETCH**, while the constraints governing it become mandatory only if that STRETCH capability is implemented.

---

## 8.11 Most expressive value of the base belongs to SPEC-13

The mobile base remains highly valuable to Makad's character.

That value is simply concentrated elsewhere.

Large, intentional movements such as:

- turning toward someone,
- backing away in surprise,
- approaching curiously,
- rotating during a performance,
- and deliberate repositioning

operate at scales above the minimum controllable-speed problem and have explicit behavioral purpose.

They therefore belong to the authored performance layer.

Audio/safety scheduling may still constrain *when* those movements can physically occur, but the B2/B3 limitations do not erase their expressive value in the way they constrain an optional always-on stochastic idle channel.

The procedural base layer is only a **marginal addition on top of the much more valuable authored locomotion capability**.

It should consequently be implemented after:

1. display-only idle,
2. normal drivetrain/navigation,
3. authored base performances,
4. and higher-value expressive channels

are already working.

g_\text{base}=0
\]

and may make procedural base idle rare during normal waking use.

35. Hazard proximity inhibits procedural base motion **before** the normal SPEC-16 safety clamp region.

36. Initial extra procedural proximity margin is:

\[
50\text{ mm}
\]

**PROVISIONAL pending sensor/stopping bench and observer testing**.

37. The existing SPEC-12 arousal scalar \(a(t)\) controls procedural base event rate/amplitude. No base-specific affect scalar is permitted.

38. Sleep forces:

\[
g_\text{base}=0
\]

with zero procedural wheel events.

39. The non-periodicity requirement applies to procedural base micro-moves but continues not to prohibit authored SPEC-13 locomotion assets.

40. The expressive value of the mobile base is expected to reside primarily in **authored performance behavior**, not in procedural idle; procedural base idle should be built last.
