### Lesson 2 — circuit, from first principles (2026-11-02)

A PCB power board is easier to reason about as separate energy paths with explicit gates. A node name is a contract: `BATBUS` is pack energy at the fuse-side star, `OPBUS` is pack energy after the user-controlled operating switch, `VSNK` is adapter energy after PCB-13 has negotiated and switched it, and `SYS` is the BQ25798's system output. They may sit at similar voltages, but they are not interchangeable nets.

```text
USB-C adapter → PCB-13 PD + input FET → VSNK → BQ25798 → BAT / BATP → fused pack entry
                                                    └→ SYS → adapter-enabled gate → CHGBUS → PCB-04
pack → fuse-side star → BATBUS → Q_OP (LTC2954 latch) → OPBUS → PCB-04
                                             └──────────────→ sense + ENERGY_OK
VSNK → charge-present hardware → CHARGE_ABSENT / Q_dis / Q_kill
J2-9 button → LTC2954 PB; LTC2954 INT → clear SYSTEM_ARM and request C2 shutdown
```

The BQ25798 has an internal battery-to-`SYS` path, so `SYS` can be live from the pack even when no adapter is attached. The charge-only gate keeps that battery-backed node from energizing `CHGBUS` in `OFF` or `OPERATE`. PCB-02 sends `OPBUS` and `CHGBUS` as separate power/return pairs to PCB-04; the destination's reverse-blocking source paths keep charge-only power from back-feeding `OPBUS`. This is what permits C2 and the display to run from the adapter during `CHARGE` while the robot's operating bus remains off.

The operating path is separate. The LTC2954 remembers the momentary button state and drives the gate of `Q_OP`, a P-channel FET from `BATBUS` to `OPBUS`. The series permit chain on PCB-03 decides whether motor energy may pass; PCB-02 supplies the independent `CHARGE_ABSENT`, `ENERGY_OK`, and `SYSTEM_ARM` terms. A valid adapter immediately withdraws the charge-absent term so motor permission opens. `Q_kill` pulls LTC2954 `KILL` low and commands `Q_OP` off promptly, including during a button press in `CHARGE`. The roughly 3 s `Q_dis` hard block is a redundant path still to be captured and tested; it is not a three-second permission to keep `OPBUS` live.

The charger is a third path. `VSNK` feeds the BQ25798's `VBUS` and, by strap, both `VAC` pins. The device's internal switching bridge and the external 2.2 µH inductor move energy into the `BAT`/`SYS` outputs. `PROG` sets 2S, 1 A and 750 kHz without firmware; `ILIM_HIZ` limits input draw; the cell NTC at `TS` gates charging by temperature. `BATP` is a separate high-impedance sense connection returned through 100 Ω to the same fuse-side pack-entry point as `BAT`, so charge current through the fuse, harness and protector does not appear as a false pack-voltage change.

| State | Charger / `SYS` | Operating path | Logic and motor result |
|---|---|---|---|
| `OFF` | BQ25798 remains in low-current battery-only mode; `SYS` may be battery-live | LTC2954 is off; `Q_OP` leaves `OPBUS` open | `Q_sys` is open, so no C2/display logic rail; PCB-03 permit chain is inactive |
| `OPERATE` | Charger remains autonomous; no adapter input | Button latch closes `Q_OP`; `OPBUS` feeds operating loads | Logic runs from `OPBUS`; motors still require every PCB-03 permit term |
| `CHARGE` | `VSNK` powers the BQ25798; gated `SYS` is sent on `CHGBUS` | Charge-present path withdraws `CHARGE_ABSENT`; `Q_kill` commands `Q_OP` off promptly; delayed `Q_dis` is the backup hard block | C2/display run for indication; a button press cannot latch `OPERATE`; motors are inhibited |

The button press has two time scales. In `OPERATE`, LTC2954 `INT` drops after about 32 ms, so hardware clears `SYSTEM_ARM` and removes motor permission without waiting for firmware. C2 starts its orderly shutdown only after the press stays low for 1 s. Holding about 5.2 s invokes the latch's hardware force-off if C2 is hung. `R_WET` raises pressed-contact current from the LTC2954's internal-pull-up 19 µA to about 0.84 mA; the open switch consumes no `R_WET` current when released. This is a power button with a safety-stop function; there is no E-stop in this build.

**Lesson 2 decisions.** The read-only RP-02 `board-specs.md` has two `SYS`-gate descriptions: §4.1 shows a gated P-FET plus Schottky OR with `OPBUS`, while §6.3 describes a `TPS22810` gate to the separate `C2B`/`DISB` inputs and PCB-04 reverse-blocking e-fuses. The fixed harness sends separate `OPBUS` and `CHGBUS` rails. Use separate `OPBUS` and adapter-gated `CHGBUS`, leaving source ORing at PCB-04 as §6.3 describes. Carry 5.7 V falling / 6.4 V rising as provisional `ENERGY_OK` values, with the EN-02/03 calculation and bench validation as release gates.

Second, the RP-02 board-spec proposal is 5.7 V falling (5.53–5.87 V), 6.4 V rising, 1 ms falling qualification and at least 100 ms clear dwell, sensed on loaded `OPBUS`. `power-boards.md` carries the 5.7 V value forward. **Comparator candidate — not yet frozen.** Microchip MIC833 is a useful first-principles fit: one internal 1.24 V reference feeds two comparators and a latch, so three resistors program independent high and low trip points, and its active-high open-drain `OUT` naturally holds the energy permit low during a detected undervoltage. The device is 1.5–5.5 V powered, 1 µA typical, specified to ±1.5% threshold accuracy, and in a 5-pin SOT-23. Datasheet caveats matter: on power-up the output can initialize high when the input is between thresholds, and the output is only guaranteed low while supply remains at least 1.2 V. With VDD lost, the open-drain output is high impedance; a pull-up to a still-live rail would therefore assert `ENERGY_OK` unless the receiving PCB-03 input has a fail-low pull-down or the pull-up and comparator supply collapse together and the receiver itself defaults low. Confirm this entire boundary, including startup and brownout, before selecting the part.

The 2026-11-02 sourcing snapshot is inconclusive for production choice: LCSC lists `MIC833YM5-TR` as C626227 with 125 in stock; JLCPCB lists `MIC833BM5` as C3658800, but that listing showed out of stock and is a different orderable suffix. No exact element14 India product listing or India warehouse count was found for either orderable MPN. Thus MIC833 is a circuit candidate, not a released sourcing choice. The threshold resistor calculation also remains pending the final comparator and reference tolerances.

**Lesson 2 decision — receiver default.** PCB-03 will add a physical pull-down at its `ENERGY_OK` receiver. This makes an open signal wire and a collapsed `+5V_C2` pull-up fail inactive. The pull resistor values remain coupled across the harness and will be checked against the actual receiving thresholds, leakage and high-state margin during the schematic/netlist pass. This pull-down does not solve loss of the comparator's local 3.3 V LDO while `+5V_C2` remains live: an unpowered open-drain comparator releases the line, so the remote pull-up wins. The PCB-03 README records the receiver requirement and this boundary.

**Lesson 2 architecture choice (2026-11-02).** Preserve the 3.3 V comparator supply and open-drain output. Add a second, independent open-drain sink at the PCB-02 source, powered from `+5V_C2` and sensing the local 3.3 V rail. Wire the two sinks together so either low-voltage detection or loss of comparator supply forces `ENERGY_OK` low; the PCB-03 receiver pull-down covers an open signal wire and loss of `+5V_C2`. This is the chosen fail-inactive boundary. A TI `TPS3808G30DBVT` is a candidate for that source-side supervisor: the TI datasheet gives a 2.79 V falling sense threshold, open-drain active-low reset, 1.7–6.5 V supply, and a selectable delay. Set its `CT` for a clear delay of at least 100 ms; verify its delay tolerance and the comparator's response time before fixing the value. The reset output becomes undefined below the supervisor's own power-on threshold, so the PCB-03 pull-down is still required when `+5V_C2` collapses.

MIC833's documented initialization needs an explicit reset if `OPBUS` starts between the provisional 5.7 V falling and 6.4 V rising levels. A candidate is to use the supervisor's active-low `RESET` to clamp MIC833 `LTH` below its 1.24 V reference during the reset delay. Once released, the latch stays low until `HTH` crosses 6.4 V. **The reset node must remain isolated from MIC833 `OUT`**: tying the clamp to their shared `ENERGY_OK` node would let MIC833's own low output hold `LTH` low indefinitely. The schematic must use separate reset and signal nodes, then prove that the supervisor can also pull `ENERGY_OK` below PCB-03's VIL without letting MIC833's low output feed back into `LTH`. Device selection, isolation topology, clamp leakage/current, and worst-case startup timing remain to be calculated before this block is captured. The `TPS3808G30DBVT` sourcing snapshot: [TI datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps3808.pdf), [LCSC C702171](https://www.lcsc.com/product-detail/C702171.html) (51 shown in stock), [JLCPCB C702171](https://jlcpcb.com/partdetail/TexasInstruments-TPS3808G30DBVT/C702171) (190 shown in stock). **Charger teaching note — why the hot loop controls placement.** The BQ25798 contains the switching FETs; the external inductor and capacitors complete its buck-boost energy-transfer paths. Every switching edge sends a short pulse of current through a capacitor, an internal switch, the SW node/inductor and the return. The small, fast loops radiate and ring if their conductors are spread apart. TI's layout priority is therefore: SYS bypass first, then PMID, then VBUS; keep each 0.1 µF capacitor closest and return it directly on the top layer; place L1 close to SW1/SW2; then BAT caps, bootstrap/REGN bypass, and thermal vias. Keep `BATP` away from both SW nodes. The 0.1 µF loops and SW copper are short/high-frequency geometry; the wide power paths carry the lower-frequency load current. Do not solve one by making all copper areas huge: oversized SW copper increases capacitive coupling and radiated noise. TI's reference layout sends SW1/SW2 through vias under the IC to an inner layer so the small bypass capacitors can remain on the top side.

The TI package is RQM0029A, a 29-pin 4 × 4 mm QFN with exposed thermal/ground pad. Pin mapping to check against the exact datasheet revision at schematic capture: 1 STAT; 2–3 VBUS; 4 BTST1; 5 REGN; 6 D+; 7 D−; 8 VAC2; 9 VAC1; 10 ACDRV2; 11 ACDRV1; 12 QON; 13 CE; 14 SCL; 15 SDA; 16 TS; 17 ILIM_HIZ; 18 BATP; 19 BTST2; 20 PROG; 21 INT; 22–23 BAT; 24 SDRV; 25 SYS; 26 SW2; 27 GND; 28 SW1; 29 PMID; exposed pad GND. Use a project footprint derived from TI's RQM0029A land-pattern drawing if the stock pattern cannot be proved identical. Add a ground thermal-via array under the exposed pad, with via count/diameter and paste segmentation set against the fab rules. Do not route the hot loop by eye around the courtyard: first reserve the capacitor positions, then the two SW escapes and inductor, and check the land pattern/courtyards and solder-mask web against the board's rule file.

**`ENERGY_OK` sizing and isolation.** The [Microchip MIC833 datasheet](https://ww1.microchip.com/downloads/aemDocuments/documents/MSLD/ProductDocuments/DataSheets/MIC833-Comparator-and-Reference-with-Adjustable-Hysteresis-DS20006336A.pdf) gives `VREF = 1.240 V` (1.221–1.259 V) and `VLO = VREF(R1+R2+R3)/(R2+R3)`, `VHI = VREF(R1+R2+R3)/R3`. **`INT_PB` isolation defect found.** The first draft used R22 = 0 Ω to join the LTC2954 open-drain button interrupt directly to `ARM_CLR_N`. **Falling filter first pass.** C33 = 47 nF from MIC833 `LTH` to ground uses the ladder's 169.9 kΩ Thevenin resistance, giving an 8.0 ms nominal RC.
